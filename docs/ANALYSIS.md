Milestone B7. Tables between `<!-- table:... -->` markers are generated by

## 1. Two-tower ablations (three seeds each)

Each variant changes one setting of the main two-tower configuration and is trained with seeds
0, 1 and 2; values are mean ± standard deviation over the seeds of exact full-catalog retrieval
metrics. Ablations are analysis only: none of them fed back into the main model, whose
configuration was fixed before they ran.

<!-- table:ablations -->
| Variant | Seeds | Test Recall@10 | Test NDCG@10 | Test Recall@50 | Test coverage@10 | Validation NDCG@10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| in_batch_logq (default) | 3 | 0.1218 ± 0.0018 | 0.0618 ± 0.0003 | 0.3310 ± 0.0027 | 0.5147 ± 0.0125 | 0.0780 ± 0.0034 |
| in_batch (no log-Q) | 3 | 0.0914 ± 0.0036 | 0.0470 ± 0.0018 | 0.2543 ± 0.0055 | 0.9552 ± 0.0157 | 0.0568 ± 0.0027 |
| uniform sampled | 3 | 0.1284 ± 0.0039 | 0.0651 ± 0.0020 | 0.3492 ± 0.0030 | 0.6015 ± 0.0295 | 0.0842 ± 0.0011 |
| mixed (in-batch + uniform) | 3 | 0.1155 ± 0.0022 | 0.0588 ± 0.0011 | 0.3268 ± 0.0042 | 0.5121 ± 0.0070 | 0.0777 ± 0.0020 |
| no side features | 3 | 0.1214 ± 0.0007 | 0.0623 ± 0.0014 | 0.3301 ± 0.0029 | 0.5386 ± 0.0035 | 0.0784 ± 0.0008 |
| embedding 32 | 3 | 0.1177 ± 0.0045 | 0.0601 ± 0.0027 | 0.3210 ± 0.0040 | 0.5174 ± 0.0287 | 0.0723 ± 0.0021 |
| embedding 128 | 3 | 0.1237 ± 0.0025 | 0.0631 ± 0.0016 | 0.3326 ± 0.0027 | 0.5494 ± 0.0197 | 0.0804 ± 0.0001 |
<!-- /table:ablations -->

*Findings.* With three seeds, the standard deviation of test Recall@10 is 0.0007-0.0045, so
differences below about 0.005 are not meaningful.

1. **log-Q correction matters most.** Without it, test Recall@10 falls from 0.1218 ± 0.0018 to
   0.0914 ± 0.0036 and NDCG@10 from 0.0618 to 0.0470, while coverage jumps from 0.5147 to 0.9552.
   Uncorrected in-batch negatives punish popular movies (they appear in batches more often), so
   the model spreads its recommendations across the catalog at the expense of accuracy: the
   sampling bias described by Yi et al., observed directly.
2. **Uniform sampled negatives are the best variant here.** 1,024 uniformly sampled negatives per
   batch reach Recall@10 0.1284 ± 0.0039 and NDCG@10 0.0651 ± 0.0020, and are also best on
   validation (NDCG@10 0.0842 ± 0.0011 versus 0.0780 ± 0.0034). With only 3,706 movies, 1,024
   negatives per step cover 28% of the catalog, close to a full softmax; in-batch negatives with
   log-Q correction are the tool for catalogs far too large for that. These runs also trained
   longer before early stopping (13-21 epochs versus 9-10), so part of the gain may come from
   training dynamics. The main model keeps the brief's in-batch + log-Q configuration, fixed before
   the ablations (`DECISIONS.md`, R12).
3. **Mixed negatives did not help** (Recall@10 0.1155 ± 0.0022, below both pure strategies), a
   negative result; the correction for the mixed proposal distribution may be the weak point.
4. **Side features add nothing measurable to retrieval** (0.1214 ± 0.0007 without them): with 93
   training positives per user on average, the id embeddings and the history carry the signal.
5. **Embedding size**: 32 dimensions is worse (0.1177 ± 0.0045); 128 is slightly better than 64
   (0.1237 ± 0.0025), within about one standard deviation.

## 2. Cold start: quality by history length

Users bucketed by how many positives they had before their test target (the input available to
every model). Recall@10 per bucket and method.

<!-- table:cold_start -->
| History length (positives before test) | Users | Recall@10: Popularity | Recall@10: Implicit ALS | Recall@10: Two-tower (exact) | Recall@10: Two-tower + ranker |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3-20 | 1,040 | 0.0615 | 0.1827 | 0.2144 | 0.2558 |
| 21-50 | 1,702 | 0.0364 | 0.1110 | 0.1404 | 0.1851 |
| 51-100 | 1,414 | 0.0389 | 0.0566 | 0.0934 | 0.1542 |
| 101-250 | 1,424 | 0.0316 | 0.0414 | 0.0751 | 0.1208 |
| 251+ | 455 | 0.0242 | 0.0374 | 0.0637 | 0.1099 |
<!-- /table:cold_start -->

*Findings.* Every model is more accurate for users with short histories: the full system reaches
Recall@10 0.2558 for users with 3-20 positives and 0.1099 for users with more than 250, and even
popularity drops from 0.0615 to 0.0242. Light users tend to like popular movies next, which are
easy to predict; heavy users have already seen the popular titles (they are excluded from their
lists) and their next like lies deeper in the catalog. The two-tower model beats ALS in every
bucket, and the ranker's relative gain grows with history length: +19% for 3-20 positives (0.2144
to 0.2558) and +73% for more than 250 (0.0637 to 0.1099). Long histories dilute the two-tower model's
mean-pooled user vector, and the ranker's recency features recover what the user is into *now*.

## 3. Popularity bias

"Head" = the most popular 20% of movies by training positives. Recall@10 is computed separately
for users whose held-out movie is a head or a tail movie; coverage@10 is the share of the catalog
recommended to anyone; the Gini coefficient (0 = every movie recommended equally often, 1 = one
movie for everyone) and the mean popularity percentile (1.0 = the most popular movie) describe
how concentrated the top-10 lists are.

<!-- table:popularity -->
| Method | Recall@10, head targets | Recall@10, tail targets | Coverage@10 | Gini of recommendation counts | Mean popularity percentile of top-10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Popularity | 0.0614 | 0.0000 | 0.0310 | 0.9947 | 0.9974 |
| Implicit ALS | 0.1375 | 0.0018 | 0.2178 | 0.9298 | 0.9621 |
| Two-tower (exact) | 0.1660 | 0.0409 | 0.5013 | 0.8691 | 0.9204 |
| Two-tower + ranker | 0.2217 | 0.0759 | 0.5774 | 0.8363 | 0.8993 |
<!-- /table:popularity -->

*Findings.* The head (20% of movies) receives 74.3% of all training positives, and 64.0% of the
test users' next likes are head movies. Popularity and ALS almost never recommend a tail movie
successfully (tail Recall@10 0.0000 and 0.0018) and concentrate their lists heavily (Gini 0.9947
and 0.9298). The two-tower model reaches tail Recall@10 0.0409 with half the catalog in use
(coverage 0.5013, Gini 0.8691), and the ranker improves both head and tail recall (0.2217 and 0.0759)
while recommending less popular movies on average (mean popularity percentile 0.8993 versus 0.9974
for the popularity baseline). Popularity bias is reduced, not removed: tail targets are still
recalled about three times less often than head targets.
